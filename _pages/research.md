## Job Market Paper

<div class="res-head"> <a href="{{ site.baseurl }}/files/Thinh_Dao_JMP_Oct30_2026.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Size-dependent Subsidies and Financial Frictions: The Case of Vietnam's Lending-rate Cap for SMEs</a> </div>

<details markdown="1">
<summary style="cursor:pointer;"><em>Abstract</em></summary>

Motivated by a policy in Vietnam that capped short-term lending rates for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) in 2012, this paper develops a heterogeneous-agent general equilibrium model that integrates a size-dependent financial subsidy into a canonical framework of entrepreneurs with borrowing constraints. In the model, firms below an employment threshold rent capital at a discounted price, choosing production scale endogenously. I use micro-level data from Vietnam to document patterns in SME financing conditions around the policy and to calibrate the model. In the quantitative exercise, a 10% capital-price discount for eligible firms raises the equilibrium interest rate by 2.9% and the wage by 0.7%. The subsidy generates substantial firm-size bunching around the employment threshold, increases aggregate output and capital, and slightly reduces total factor productivity. Counterfactual experiments reveal a trade-off between scale and efficiency: higher subsidies expand capital and output and further compress capital distortions, but at the cost of higher overall misallocation. This paper connects the literature on size-dependent distortions and financial frictions by allowing the cost of capital to vary with firm size.

</details>

## Working Paper

<div class="res-head"> <a href="{{ site.baseurl }}/files/Thinh_Dao_Misallocation_in_Vietnam_Feb2026.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Misallocation in Vietnam: A Structural Analysis Across Industries and Ownership Structures</a> </div>

<details markdown="1">
<summary style="cursor:pointer;"><em>Abstract</em></summary>

In this paper, I use a comprehensive firm-level dataset and develop a structural general equilibrium model featuring three layers of economic organization: firms, ownership structures (state-owned, non-state, and foreign-invested), and industries to quantify resource misallocation in Vietnam over the 2011-2020 period. I also decompose the effect of distortions in each structural layer on aggregate productivity. The findings reveal significant allocative inefficiencies within the Vietnamese economy, that intensify over time. Distortions in the capital market have a weaker impact on aggregate productivity than those in the labor market—a pattern that persists across various aggregation levels. Counterfactual exercises show that distortions among firms within industry-ownership groups and across industries are the primary drivers of inefficiency. In contrast, distortions across ownership structures within industries have a minor effect on aggregate productivity and exhibit a modest declining trend, largely due to reductions in labor distortions.

</details>
